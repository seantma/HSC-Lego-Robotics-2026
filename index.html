Build habits

  • Accuracy before speed.
  • Check beam and axle length carefully.
  • Check left/right and front/back orientation.
  • Compare both sides of a symmetrical build.
  • Never force a Technic piece that should fit easily.
  • Find the last step where your build matched the diagram.
  • Ask your teammate what they observe before rebuilding.

Quick hardware reminder

  • Motors: normally connect to output ports A–D.
  • Sensors: normally connect to input ports 1–4.
  • Large Motor: strong drive motor.
  • Medium Motor: compact, faster mechanism motor.
  • Color Sensor: detects color / reflected light.
  • Gyro Sensor: measures rotation.
  • Touch Sensor: detects pressed / bumped / released.
  • Ultrasonic Sensor: measures distance.

🛠️ Troubleshoot

Engineers do not stop at “it doesn't work.” Use evidence to isolate the problem.

  1. Observe: What exactly happened?
  2. Expected vs. actual: What did you expect instead?
  3. Power: Is the EV3 Brick on and sufficiently charged?
  4. Ports: Are motors and sensors plugged into the expected ports?
  5. Cables: Are they fully inserted?
  6. Mechanical check: Can wheels, gears, and mechanisms move freely?
  7. Build check: Compare the robot to the instruction image.
  8. Program check: Verify block, port, power, direction, rotations/degrees, and stop behavior.
  9. Small test: Run only the smallest piece that might be failing.
  10. Teammate check: Explain the problem out loud to your partner.
  11. Documentation: Use the User Guide or lesson guide.
  12. Ask for coaching: Tell the instructor what you already tested.
A useful help request:

“We expected both motors to move forward. Motor B moves, but Motor C does not. We checked the cable and port and tested Motor C by itself.”
